ITS IC Promotion Summary

At Amazon, people are our most valued resource. Promotions play an important role in our approach to recognizing employee growth and achievement and maintaining our high-performance culture. These promotions occur quarterly for job levels up to L6 and twice a year for senior-level promotions (L7+).

We promote when the employee’s role is scoped at the next level, the employee has consistently demonstrated next-level performance and there is role available at next level. At the time of promotion, we expect they will still have some areas to grow at the next level. We promote those we believe will raise the bar over time, with the capability to grow any necessary skills through coaching and on-the-job learning. If an employee is already exceeding expectations at the next level, we may have waited too long to promote.

To improve and define the promotion process for ITSM’s and align globally. This wiki will identify the steps required for managers to follow and provide the ability for ITSMs to promote their experienced employees successfully.

Process

The below process diagram outlines the step-by-step journey of the promotion document. At each stage there is a pathway for success but also opportunities for unsuccessful documents to receive stage specific feedback. This feedback will help the author improve the document for future submission.

 

Expectations at Each Review Stage

  • Peer Review - check format, remove weasel words, check feedback providers 
  • Regional Review - check format, remove weasel words, check feedback providers, provide feedback on promo doc content, ensure doc narratives clearly explain engineer actions, behaviours and impact
  • GSD2 Review  - Consult the L4 levelling guidelines to ensure adequate examples of individual showcasing they are operating at L4 bar
  • L6 Panel Review - content to be added when framework created

Expectations of the Review Panel

  • A leader who has had 2 or more successful promotion documents approved
  • Ensure cross regional reviews are held
  • Be familiar with STAR Writing Format

 

GSD2 Review Process for 2024

When your document is approved regionally it then moves to GSD2 review. At this stage, the panel will review the document inline with the Role Guidelines for L4 (listed below)

  • You are able to troubleshoot and resolve straightforward IT problems without SOPs. You escalate appropriately when work significantly deviates from documented practices or when barriers arise.
  • You are executing small projects that deliver value to your team and/or customers. You are able define requirements and manage the execution. These projects may involve other locations or be part of a larger continuous improvement.
  • You are writing and coordinating CMs. You no longer need a template.
  • You are trusted with higher levels of permissions to perform systems management procedures, make small code changes, etc.
  • You identify root-causes of operational issues and process inefficiencies. You may be automating tasks, modifying small tools, and/or proposing new SOPs.  
  • You make appropriate tradeoffs: your time available vs. issue severity/SLA vs. support work volume (e.g., tickets, CM, projects, etc.).
  • You author operating procedures and knowledge base articles.

When writing your examples, you want to focus Leadership Principles, Metrics, Contributions, and Impact. 

The below list highlights the key components to strengthen an example.

Principles of a Strong Example+

  • A demonstration of technical ability.
  • An ability to Deep Dive to solve a complex problem, and work without SOPs. How the engineer solved the problem is clearly explained.
  • Cross-team collaboration. Working with partner teams, including Tier 2 and/or service owners to resolve the issue.
  • An ability to Think Big, beyond the immediate customer. The engineer scaled the solution through a KB/IT update, training, or other communication as applicable.
  • Where the engineer has scripting skills, showing Invent and Simplify by creating a script to solve a problem, and working with SET or other partner teams to improve existing tooling, or scale their solution.
  • Training and mentoring.
  • Mentoring a peer who is struggling with one or more metrics. How the engineer helped their colleague is clearly documented. The explanation includes the steps they took to identify root cause issues; how they put together a plan of action, identifying resources; and how they put that together into a structure to support the peer or new hire. The example shows sustained improvements in metrics or other measurable behaviours.
  • If working with partner teams, such as L&D to deliver training, the example includes contributions the engineer made to help improve the training content and quality. The impact on trainees is quantified wherever possible, such as through metric results or feedback surveys.
  • If the engineer is a team leader or has been given responsibilities to train in team meetings, the example clearly documents the steps they took to identify pain points on their team. It explains how they gathered information on topics to teach, how they constructed trainings and researched resources, and the impact the training had on the team. If these are broader knowledge gaps, the example explains how the engineer sought to scale the results of their training to others beyond their immediate team.

Global Service Desk 2

Job Description: 

As a ITS Support Eng I (GSD 2), you carry an elevated technical responsibility focused on providing high-quality support for computer hardware, operating systems, and enterprise application software issues across Amazon's customer base. Your support scope extends beyond individual users or specific groups, encompassing all corporate customers.

You are able to evaluate and prioritize issues based on the severity, time frame, and volume of other expected work, to meet SLAs. You may resolve independently or take the appropriate steps to transfer to the service owner team, as and when required. You connect with customers, while also providing real-time support to peers from GSD1 and On-Prem. You are the end of the road support for single-user issues, before reaching to service owners. You identify possible process improvement areas and outline drivers for change; you support and enhance the ongoing career development of colleagues and represent their team successfully on global and regional initiatives.

You are reflective and evaluative when actions require significant deviation and/or escalation from SOPs, when issues become risks, and when projects hit barriers. You understand and drive constructive technical discussion, asking questions that are appropriate and purposeful to the greater benefit of the business. You are collaborative and goal orientated; you are a skilled problem solver, trusted with a higher level of systems permissions to positively complete tasks to the satisfaction of all customers and stakeholders.

Key Responsibilities+

  • Contact support - You are capable of handling the most complex issues with the GSD business while also assisting GSD1 with overflow contacts during high volumes.
  • Assist - You will guide and mentor engineers in troubleshooting, providing assistance and direction for issue resolution, within the Assist queue.
  • Escalated contacts - You will take ownership of escalated issues, resolving complex problems efficiently, and handling permissions-gated issues.
  • Executive Support - You will provide Executive Support for L8+ Customers, through skills-based routing via chat and phone.
  • Repeat Contact Resolution - Responsible for resolving repeat customer issues, auditing initial interactions, and providing feedback to ensure continuous improvement.
  • Hiring, training and mentorship - You will participate in hiring and developing the best individuals, in order to support the continuous growth and improvement of GSD team. You will offer mentorship to support L3 engineers’ technical development.
  • Process improvement - You will be involved in continuous improvement efforts to support GSD’s king pin goals, supporting projects requiring technical expertise sourced from engineers.

 

Promotion Writing Format

The review should utilize the most recent template from promo website. 

Scope of Role

At Amazon, we promote when : the employee’s role is scoped at the next level, the employee has consistently demonstrated that they will be successful in that role and there is role available at next level. The Scope of role section of the promotion document is your chance to provide evidence that the employee’s role is scoped at the next level.

L2 to L3+

You support live customer contacts from Amazonians globally. You have technical knowledge in order to apply basic application-level troubleshooting for common technology domains (e.g., end-user hardware/software, basic networking, VPN, operating systems). In your troubleshooting activity you are able to follow SOPs and to have slight deviations from SOPs when the job requires. You are up to date with Amazon policy and apply it in your interaction with customers. You are able to triage and escalate any system or network outage to reduce downtime.
During an interaction with a customer you are able to direct and guide the customer to a self-service solution. If the customer doesn’t have the knowledge to follow the self-service solution, they are able to identify this and offer guidance or connect directly to the customer via remote assistance software. If no self-service solution is available you are able to locate the required SOP and resolve clearly defined technical problems, which may involve slight deviations. If a customer is contacting you with an IT issue that is not supported by us, you offer them guidance and point them in the right direction in order to solve the issue.
You inform customers of needed repairs and answer basic questions. You ensure that customers understand and are satisfied with the work completed. You follow the instructions received in “Chat Etiquette” and “Phone Etiquette” training's. As an Amazonian, your responsibility is to be Customer Obsessed. You create and submit detailed call logs documenting customer interactions that are accurate, thorough, and timely.
You will improve the team by contributing to the creation and sharing of knowledge by providing SOP feedback, and participating in continuous improvement efforts in a team environment. You will help onboard new team members and assist them when they encounter unexpected situations. You escalate when situations require significant deviation from SOPs or when issues appear risky or difficult.
You are able to participate in small projects with supervised instruction. You understand that following clear instructions and providing updates to stakeholders is key for a successful project. When presented with a roadblock, you are able to escalate appropriately.

L3 to L4+

As an IT Support Engineer I within Global Service Desk (GSD) you have increased technical responsibility in relation to customer support. The support or the solution you offer within role, is not limited to a single user or a group but to all of Amazons corporate customers. You provide high quality support for computer hardware, operating systems & enterprise application software issues. Additionally, you have the scope and expertise to troubleshoot and resolve straightforward problems that may not yet have a Standard Operating Procedure (SOPs).
As an IT Support Engineer I, within GSD you are able to evaluate issues based on the severity, time frame, and volume of other expected work to meet SLAs. You may resolve independently or take the appropriate steps to devolve to a co-worker or another team, as and when required. You can identify possible project areas and outline drivers for change; you support and enhance the ongoing career long professional development of colleagues and represent their team successfully on regional initiatives.
Overarching, you strive to hire and develop the best individuals, in order to support the continuous growth and improvement of the GSD team. You are reflective, evaluative and appreciate when actions require significant deviation and/or escalation from SOPs, when issues become risks, and when projects hit barriers. You, as an individual understand and are able to drive constructive technical discussion, asking questions that are appropriate and purposeful to the greater benefit of the business. You are collegiate, collaborative and goal orientated; you are skilled problem solver, trusted with a higher level of systems permissions to positively execute tasks to the satisfaction of all stakeholders.

L4 to L5+

The IT Support Engineer II (ITSE II) balances their work-week supporting tactical and strategic initiatives across the Global Service Desk (GSD) business, supporting managers with escalations, providing technical mentoring, and acting as the operational bridge between stakeholders and GSD. The ITSE II will partner with subsidiary IT teams to learn about their IT department business operations. They will utilize that information to contribute to roadmaps and develop processes for a smooth integration of their customers into an Amazon computing environment. An ITSE II will create and maintain relationships with teams such as ITOPM, Learning and Development (L&D), Enterprise Engineering (EE), and Kumo. The ITSE II role also makes improvements to Amazon IT tools, systems and workflows. This individual raises the technical bar by being able to troubleshoot independently and is considered a subject matter expert.

This individual raises the technical bar within ITS by maintaining and growing their technical skills, including keeping abreast of developments in AWS IT products and services. They are both technical and are role models regarding our leadership principles. Uses technical knowledge and skills to decide which actions to take and in what priority order. The ITSE II supports managers, stakeholders and project owners to seek out trends that impact multiple customers. They assist in Problem Management (PB), identifying root causes to defer and reduce contacts. (e.g., contact trends, AHT / contact reduction).

The scope of work will require an engineer to take ownership of ambiguous tasks such as understanding subsidiary business operations, creating roadmaps, deep diving into data, problem solving, documentation, and light program management. It will be the engineer’s responsibility to determine which tasks take priority within multiple projects without any guidance. They will need to be able to work well independently and collaboratively with teammates and stakeholders. An engineer will be measured by their ability to work with internal and external partner teams, use data to improve GSD processes, and complete projects within established deadlines. The team’s accomplishments will have a global impact and drive successes of IT Services.

 

 

Promotion Assessment

When writing the review using the Star Format (Situation, Task, Action, Result), it is important to base the examples on the scope of the role. Data should also be used in the examples to support the situation and result. The name format for the examples should be the name of achievement in bold, followed bellow by their LP's displayed in italics.

The STAR Method

S

Situation
20%

Explain the situation so that your interviewer understands the context of your example, they do not need to know every detail!

T

Task
10%

Next, talk about the task, problem, or challenge that you took responsibility for completing, or the goal of your efforts.

A

Action
60%

Describe the actions that you personally took to complete the task or reach the end goal. Highlight skills or character traits addressed in the question.

R

Result
10%

Explain the positive outcomes or results of your actions or efforts. Here, it is important to highlight quantifiable results.

What Good Looks Like:

Example 1+

Making Great Hiring Decisions #hire and develop the best #insist on highest standards
Due to the shift to remote work and an increased demand for IT support, the organization needed to expand its IT support team. The hiring process was critical as SLAs were low, impacting overall metrics and customer experience. To address this, a strong hiring team was established in the XYZ site.
Employee XYZ was selected to become an interviewer, thanks to their excellent communication skills and strong evaluation abilities. XYZ was tasked with conducting interviews for the position of IT Services Support Specialist - L3 at the XYZ site.
Employee XYZ has already participated in 13 phone screens and 21 on-site interviews, providing valuable feedback during debriefs and helping to hire top talent that aligns with the company's leadership principles. XYZ remains actively involved in talent recruitment in the XYZ site.

Example 2+

Ticket as a channel (TAAC) training. #Hire and develop the best #Earn trust
A new project has been introduced to IT Services called "Ticket as a Channel" in 2021. The scope of this project was to introduce engineers with a new approach, working on live tasks and handling tickets as live contacts in Omnia. Ticket as a Channel will route tickets in a live approach so customers get their issues routed to the first engineer available or the previous engineer who handled their existing ticket based on engineer’s availability, and priority defined by Workforce Management (WFM)
Employee XYZ received training on handling tickets through Omnia and quickly assimilated the information. Within two weeks of hands-on experience with TAAC, XYZ took on a leadership role by passing along their knowledge to their colleagues. This included providing 4 training sessions and participating in Q&A discussions.
As a result of XYZ's efforts, 16 engineers from the EMEA region participated in these training sessions and went on to work on their TaaC profile. This led to a reduction in the number of tickets in the queue, with 8160 tasks being handled by EMEA engineers so far. Most of these engineers received training from Employee XYZ, further demonstrating their impact on the team.

 

What Makes a Strong Promo Doc?

Leverage these best practices to write a promo doc that articulates a builder’s ability to deliver at the next
level.

Writing Guidance (The Do's)

AttributesExplanation/Example
Leadership Principles  Review this resource to calibrate which behaviors demonstrate strong LP performance. Keep your
examples simple and focused. Example: “Recognizing that we lacked customer insight, Alex set up eight focus groups to
ensure we made informed decisions. [Bias for Action]”
 Metrics  Replace adjectives and weasel words (nearly all, significantly better) with data; provide relevant
benchmarks. Be consistent with how you describe measurable accomplishments. Example: “As a result of Huan’s drive to
partner with another technical team, we reduced server side tp90 latency from 10ms to 1ms.”
 Contributions  Use active voice and tie specific actions back to the builder. Articulate each significant
task/milestone/tradeoffs of the builder’s contribution. Reference Role Leveling Guidelines. Example: “Kendi developed an
SOP which defined the scope of repairs and documented standards.”
 Impact  Think about broader, scalable impact. How did this effort influence other projects, teams, and organizations?
How did it impact AWS? Example: “Due to Rahi’s leadership, this product became available in a new country in Q1, with
more than 50K new customers in the first month. “

Promo Doc Excerpt - Version 1 [Good]+

A few months after starting on the team, Casey designed a new process for sharing information with our partner teams. After meeting with multiple stakeholders, Casey identified a technical resource and they worked together to design a new process and implement it across the team. This automated process design saves our team many hours per week of manual labor and allows partner teams to receive the data more frequently. This new process has been implemented across four teams in our organization.

Promo Doc Excerpt – Version 2 [Better]+

1= Leadership Principles | 2 = Metrics | 3 = Contributions | 4 = Impact
Upon discovering an inefficient process for information sharing with our partner team, Casey designed and socialized a new process and implemented it three months ahead of schedule.2 Earlier this year, our central data team was redistributed across the organization, as a result, a data improvement process was reprioritized and our team hurriedly stood up a new process that we’ve used for the past six months. Once he took on the process, Casey identified the inefficiencies and met with 15 key stakeholders to understand how the data is processed to design a better solution [Learn and Be Curious].1 & 3
Leveraging their background in process design, Casey identified a technical partner to create an internal tool3 to automate data sharing, delivering key insights to our partner teams every week (vs. biweekly) [Bias for Action, Invent and Simplify].1 After socializing this design more broadly, Casey implemented this new process which collectively saved 10 hours per week across 3 teams.2 They also socialized this new process with other Product Managers 4 and to date, it has been implemented by 4 other teams, saving 40+ hours across key technical teams in our organization.2 & 4

What makes a stronger promodoc?

Leverage these best practices to write a compelling promo doc to give your builder their best chance at success.

Writing Practices To Avoid (The Don’ts)

AttributesExplanation/Example
Leadership Principles Avoid listing multiple LPs within one example. Don’t feel obligated to use every LP or reference the same LP multiple times.
Metrics Refrain from using different metrics to describe the same accomplishment. Be careful not to overwhelm the audience with data.
Contributions Avoid passive language and team-level contributions. Example: “The initiative was owned by multiple people across the team.”
Impact Refrain from only describing impact at the team-level. Don’t generalize or hypothesize and keep the impact focused on facts.

Promotion Feedback Providers

Promotions require feedback providers to support the promotion candidate and confirm their actions demonstrate next level performance. The persons providing feedback will need to write a short supporting narrative about "Reasons to Promote" and "Reasons to Not Promote" the candidate within the Promote Tool, hence they should have adequate exposure to and familiarity with the candidate's sustained performance at the next level. Feedback providers should consist of 4-6 persons whom are at L4 or above for any L3 to L4 promotions. For L2 to L3 promotions, the requirement is 3-4 supporters at L3 or above. The official feedback request should be made through the Amazon Promote Tool found in Ivy but it's recommended to touch base with proposed supporters in advance to confirm they are inclined and have sufficient data to provide feedback

Additional Information

The last 6-12 months metrics should be included in the additional information , including an explanation of any misses. The manager should provide feedback on the engineer's performance, with a recommendation of 200 words or less. Additionally, the manager should share 2-3 examples of positive feedback received by the engineer, such as accolades or thank-you emails. The review should also include a section on rewards and recognition, highlighting the efforts of the engineer. If there are any relevant links that support the examples given, they should be included as well.

 

Frequently Asked Questions (ITSE)

Check here for frequently asked engineer questions and answers to aid in development conversations.

What are the general criteria required for promotion?+

We promote when the employee’s role is scoped at the next level, the employee has consistently demonstrated next-level performance and there is role available at next level.

How do I express my interest in a promotion?+

Speak with your manager during your GROW 1:1 meetings. Your manager should identify any gaps in your performance at current level and articulate any areas where you need to work. You should have examples and data to support that you have been performing at the next level to align on your readiness.

What steps are involved for a promotion process?+

Upon aligning with your manager on your readiness for promotion, they will begin writing the promotion document. Your manager may require additional context and data points on your accomplishments and contributions to show impact and next level performance. Once drafted, the doc will be reviewed by multiple review panels and teams including a regional promo panel, GSD2 panel, an L6 Regional Mgr panel, and finally your skip-level manager. As a promotion document moves through the process, your manager may require additional data. In addition to the promotion document, feedback providers are also required to provide short narratives for reasons to and not to promote.

How often do promotion opportunities become available?+

Promotion evaluations take place quarterly. It is suggested to begin drafting a promotion document well in advance of the targeted promo quarter to allow time for your promotion document to receive feedback, perform revisions, and allow feedback providers sufficient time to write their narratives.

What specific skills or experience should I focus on developing to be a strong candidate for a promotion?+

To show promotion readiness, you should possess strong metrics, positive LP behaviours, delivered impact in your accomplishments, and have examples of demonstrating the job role guideline with consistency. Examples showing impact beyond a single team, solving issues upstream or at scale, working with partner teams, and leading initiatives are great experiences to have and showcase skills in action.

How can I demonstrate initiative and take on projects aligned with my skills needed to level up?+

Work with your peers and manager to express interest in topics that align to your strengths and share ideas for improving areas that would make an impact for the organization. If you observe something that should be improved or fixed, discuss the opportunity with your manager and peers to explore if the topic is already being addressed and could use help or propose new projects based on your strengths and past experiences.

What is the process of requesting feedback on my performance with regards to promotion readiness?+

These discussions should take place with your manager during your GROW 1:1 meetings. Your manager should identify any gaps in your performance at current level, and articulate any areas where you need to work. They should work with you to create development plans and also share ratings on your monthly performance.

If I’m not selected for a promotion, what resources are available to create my development plan for the future promotion?+

Work with your manager to form an updated GROW plan and goals to demonstrate promotion readiness. In addition to your growth discussions, review the resources and content shared on the ITS Promotion Process wiki to improve the promotion document and narrative. Use previously identified areas of opportunity as topics to demonstrate growth and improvement in.
